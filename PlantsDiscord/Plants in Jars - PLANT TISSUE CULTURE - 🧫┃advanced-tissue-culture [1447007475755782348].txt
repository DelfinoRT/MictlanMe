I think its going to be plant specific and since I was working from callus thats continually multiplying the original explant material (cotyledon) was not there as it either calluses or gets subcultured away within the 1st few subcultures. 

I would only subculture the best of the best. If a plate had 16 clumps of calli I'd only take the most active growth with tight globular cells. Elongated structures or any decay would be removed- 1:1 ratio transferred (i had an over abundance)

If you're keeping plant instead of callus in TC- things will be different too. 

Disadvantages of using old callus that I saw was stunted growth, sterility (no flowers or seeds..sometimes flowers and dud pods where the seed early germinates in its pod.), and of course just the weird phenotypes where leaves or plant structure looked funky compared to other plants grown. 

When working with calli the age of the stock material can be two years...then you try to turn that into a plant and add another several months to a year to get a plantlet ready for greenhouse life so then you're looking at a life cycle with multiple stress points that have created an opportunity for funky stuff.
In the world of wanting somal clonal variations- older calli could help.

I did a little more googling. This was shamelessly ripped from an ai overview. It's some books you can buy or maybe rent depending on your local library 
For books with callus production recipes, focus on practical guides like "Plant Tissue Culture: Techniques and Experiments" (Park), "Plant Cell and Tissue Culture" (Pollard & Walker), "Plant Propagation by Tissue Culture" (George, Hall, De Klerk), and "Plant Cell Culture Protocols", as these provide hands-on protocols, media recipes (often variations of MS media with specific hormones like auxins/cytokinins for callus induction), and step-by-step instructions for beginners to advanced users. 
Key Books to Look For:
Plant Tissue Culture: Techniques and Experiments (by Sunghun Park): Known for classroom-tested experiments, media prep, and current techniques for regeneration and morphogenesis, ideal for practical application.
Plant Cell and Tissue Culture (by Pollard & Walker): Praised for its step-by-step recipes and practical guidance, making it great for all experience levels.
Plant Propagation by Tissue Culture (by George, Hall, & De Klerk): A comprehensive guide that covers fundamental aspects, including media and culture initiation.
Plant Cell Culture Protocols (edited by Loyola-Vargas & Ochoa-Alejo): A collection of protocols that often details specific recipes and methods for various culture types, including callus.
Plant Tissue Culture: Theory and Practice (by Bhojwani & Razdan): A classic that covers basics, media prep, and applications, great for foundational knowledge. 
What to Expect in These Books:
Media Recipes: Detailed lists of macronutrients, micronutrients, vitamins, sugars, and growth regulators (like 2,4-D, NAA for auxins; Kinetin, BAP for cytokinins) to induce callus.
Hormonal Ratios: Specific auxin-to-cytokinin ratios that trigger callus formation versus shoot/root development.
Step-by-Step Protocols: Instructions for explant preparation, sterilization, media making, and culture initiation.
